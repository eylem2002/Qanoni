The successful companies overwhelmingly follow the hybrid approach — and it's not close. Across virtually every serious contract drafting platform (Sirion, Spellbook, Ironclad, the rest of the 2026 Gartner-leader set), the pattern is the same: pre-approved templates and clause libraries form the foundation of consistent contract drafting, with AI-powered drafting layered on top to generate language using prompts and deal inputs while staying aligned with approved standards. Spellbook puts it plainly too — their system lets you generate contracts from templates or from scratch, but the core workflow is inserting pre-approved clauses into new contracts so you're not rewriting or reconsidering terms every time.

The reasoning the industry gives for this is almost identical to what you and the other AI already worked out: the strongest tools let firms create, store, and reuse approved clauses and full templates so every agreement reflects the firm's standards and risk posture, instead of reinventing the wheel with every draft. And critically, the smartest implementations don't treat this as rigid — they adapt language based on contract type, jurisdiction, party role, and matter-specific details, which reduces the risk of mismatched clauses or missing provisions. That's the hybrid in action: fixed approved language for the boilerplate, AI adaptation for the variable parts.

There's also a trust signal worth noting: a recent industry survey found only about 22% of legal users currently have high trust in raw generative AI output, but teams with high trust report dramatically better ROI than teams without it — which tells you trust isn't built by letting the model freewheel, it's built by constraining it. The pattern holds even at the more research-heavy, bespoke end of the market (Harvey, CoCounsel, Lexis+ AI) — those tools lean more generative because their work genuinely is novel each time, but even there, generation happens on top of a retrieval layer over verified sources (precedent, case law, firm documents), not pure free-form writing.

So the honest takeaway for your system: nobody at scale is doing pure template-and-fill, and nobody trustworthy is doing pure unconstrained generation either. The companies that have actually earned lawyer trust use approved clause libraries as the spine and AI for assembly, adaptation, and the genuinely variable parts — exactly the hybrid the other AI proposed to you.
